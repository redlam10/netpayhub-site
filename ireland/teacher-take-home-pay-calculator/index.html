How teacher take-home pay works in Ireland (2026)

Teachers are paid on a national incremental salary scale and taxed as standard PAYE employees, but with one extra deduction most workers don't have: superannuation (the public-service pension), plus the Additional Superannuation Contribution (ASC). This calculator applies the 2026 income tax, USC and PRSI rules, and lets you add your pension/superannuation percentage to see your real net pay.

Teacher salary scale (2026)

A €2,000 Personal Tax Credit plus the €2,000 Employee (PAYE) Tax Credit are then subtracted. USC runs 0.5% / 2% / 3% / 8% across its bands (income at or below €13,000 is exempt), and Class A PRSI is 4.2% of gross (rising to 4.35% from 1 October 2026).

Superannuation & ASC

Because teachers are in a public-service pension scheme, pension contributions and the ASC are deducted from gross pay. The exact rates depend on your salary and whether you joined the Single Public Service Pension Scheme (most teachers since 2013) or an earlier scheme, so we don't hard-code them — enter your combined pension/superannuation percentage (it's on your payslip) to see your true take-home.

People also ask

How much does a teacher take home in Ireland?

A newly qualified teacher on €45,379 (single, before superannuation) keeps about €37,227 a year — roughly €3,102/month — after income tax (€5,352), USC (€894) and PRSI (€1,906) in 2026. Superannuation and ASC reduce this further.

What is the starting salary for a teacher in Ireland in 2026?

From 1 February 2026, €45,379 for a primary teacher appointed on/after 1 January 2011 (Point 1), or €43,341 for pre-2011 entrants. Both rise with yearly increments plus any qualification allowances.

Why is my actual teacher take-home lower than this?

Superannuation plus the Additional Superannuation Contribution (ASC) come out of teachers' pay before you receive it. Enter your pension/superannuation percentage (from your payslip) to model it.

Does this include teaching allowances?

No — it uses your basic gross. Add allowances (honours degree, posts of responsibility, Gaeltacht/island) into the gross figure for an accurate result.
